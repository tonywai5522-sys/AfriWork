# AfriWork Product Requirements Document (PRD)

## 1. Document Purpose
AfriWork is a digital talent and work infrastructure platform designed to help African youth showcase verified skills, access work opportunities, collaborate on projects, and build professional reputation. This PRD defines the product strategy, scope, user needs, and delivery plan for an enterprise-grade SaaS platform.

---

## 2. Product Overview

### Product Name
AfriWork

### Product Type
A multi-sided digital talent marketplace and work collaboration platform for African youth, employers, educators, and institutions.

### Product Category
Digital Talent Infrastructure | Freelance Marketplace | Skills Verification Platform | Work Collaboration Platform

---

## 3. Vision
To become Africa’s trusted digital talent infrastructure platform that connects skilled youth to meaningful work, opportunity, and long-term professional growth.

---

## 4. Mission
To empower African youth by providing a trusted, accessible, and scalable platform where they can build professional identities, discover opportunities, collaborate on projects, and earn income with confidence.

---

## 5. Target Users
AfriWork serves four primary user groups:

- Young professionals and freelancers seeking work opportunities
- Employers and hiring teams looking for vetted talent
- Project leaders and remote teams requiring collaboration tools
- Training institutions and organizations supporting workforce development

---

## 6. User Personas

### Persona A: The Emerging Digital Talent
- Age: 18–30
- Background: Recent graduate, self-taught developer, designer, writer, marketer, or freelancer
- Needs: A professional profile, visibility, work opportunities, trust, and payment readiness
- Pain Points: Limited access to formal networks, difficulty proving skills, low trust from employers, fragmented job search

### Persona B: The Growing Freelancer
- Age: 22–35
- Background: Independent professional with some portfolio experience
- Needs: Recurring clients, better discovery, secure collaboration, and reputation management
- Pain Points: Inconsistent income, unreliable clients, weak professional credibility, lack of project management support

### Persona C: The Employer / Hiring Manager
- Age: 28–45
- Background: Startup founder, SME owner, NGO recruiter, or remote team lead
- Needs: Fast access to trustworthy talent, transparent skill verification, collaborative workflows
- Pain Points: High hiring risk, difficulty screening candidates, fragmented communication, trust concerns

### Persona D: The Skills Ecosystem Partner
- Age: 30–50
- Background: Training institution, bootcamp, NGO, or workforce development program
- Needs: Talent pipelines, verified learner outcomes, employer connections
- Pain Points: Limited visibility into employability, weak alumni engagement, no structured bridge to jobs

---

## 7. Problems Being Solved

1. African youth often lack a trusted digital identity that can be recognized by employers and clients.
2. Many skilled individuals cannot easily prove their capabilities beyond resumes and social media.
3. Employers struggle to find qualified, trustworthy talent efficiently and securely.
4. Freelancers and professionals often operate in fragmented ecosystems with poor collaboration and payment tools.
5. Institutions lack a direct bridge from training programs to meaningful work outcomes.
6. Cross-border work opportunities are difficult to access due to trust, verification, and payment limitations.

---

## 8. Unique Value Proposition
AfriWork combines talent discovery, skills verification, professional profile building, project collaboration, and opportunity matching in a single trusted platform tailored to African talent and employers.

### Core Differentiators
- Africa-first design and market understanding
- Trust-centered profile and verification system
- Skills-based matchmaking rather than resume-only discovery
- Built-in collaboration and work management tools
- Support for global and local opportunity access
- A long-term digital work identity platform, not just a job board

---

## 9. Product Goals

### Business Goals
- Build a sustainable talent marketplace for African youth
- Increase trust and conversion between talent and employers
- Establish AfriWork as a leading digital talent infrastructure brand
- Create revenue opportunities through subscriptions, commissions, and premium services

### User Goals
- Help users create professional, credible digital identities
- Connect talent to relevant opportunities quickly
- Enable projects and teams to work securely and transparently
- Improve earning potential and job readiness

- Talent job discovery and filtering
- Application submission and tracking

### 10.4 Project Collaboration Workspace
- Create project workspaces
- Invite collaborators
- Task assignment and project tracking
- File sharing and discussion threads

### 10.5 Skills Verification Foundation
- Profile verification badges
- Certificate and portfolio upload
- Admin moderation workflow for trust signals

### 10.6 Admin and Moderation Tools
- User management
- Content moderation
- Reports and abuse controls
- Basic analytics dashboard

## 14. Competitive Analysis

| Competitor | Strengths | Weaknesses | AfriWork Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upwork | Large freelancer network | High competition, high fees, limited regional trust context | Better Africa-focused trust and local relevance |
| Fiverr | Easy listing and quick ordering | Limited depth for professional career building | Stronger long-term professional identity and verification |
| LinkedIn | Strong professional networking | Not built as a work execution platform | Combined network + work + collaboration + verification |
| Local Job Boards | Regional reach | Low trust, weak verification, poor professional tooling | More reliable talent infrastructure and collaboration features |
| Traditional Recruitment Agencies | Human screening | Costly and slow | Digital, scalable, transparent, and accessible |

### Competitive Positioning
AfriWork will position itself as a trusted, Africa-native digital work platform that combines talent visibility, verification, collaboration, and opportunity access into one ecosystem.

## 15. Functional Requirements

### User Registration and Authentication
- Users can create accounts securely and verify their identity.
- Role-based access must be enforced on all protected resources.
- Passwords and sensitive operations must follow secure standards.

### Profile Management
- Users can create and edit professional profiles.
- Profiles support skills, portfolio, work history, certifications, and availability.
- Profile completeness should encourage better onboarding.

### Opportunity Management
- Employers can create and publish jobs.
- Applicants can submit applications and track status.
- Users can filter opportunities by skill, location, type, and experience level.

### Collaboration
- Users can create workspaces, invite collaborators, and manage tasks.
- Users can share files and exchange discussion updates.

### Verification and Trust
- Admins can approve or reject verification submissions.
- Verified profiles should display trust indicators in a clear and consistent format.

### Admin Tools
- Admins must be able to monitor activity, remove harmful content, and manage user access.

---

## 16. Non-Functional Requirements

### Security
- Secure authentication and authorization
- Encryption of sensitive data
- Protection against abuse, spam, and fraud

### Performance
- Fast page load times and responsive interactions
- Scalable architecture to support growth in users and projects

### Reliability
- High availability and graceful error handling
- Monitoring and logging for operational visibility

### Accessibility
- Keyboard navigation and screen-reader support
- Responsive, inclusive, and mobile-friendly interfaces

### Compliance and Data Governance
- User privacy protection
- Clear consent and data handling policies
- Audit-friendly administrative actions

---

## 19. Risks and Mitigations

### Risk: Low trust in online talent matching
Mitigation: Prioritize identity verification, moderation, and transparent reviews.

### Risk: Poor user adoption
Mitigation: Offer simple onboarding, guided profile setup, and strong early value.

### Risk: Marketplace quality issues
Mitigation: Build strong moderation tools and clear standards.

### Risk: Payment and compliance friction
Mitigation: Introduce secure, compliant payment pathways gradually.

## 21. Recommendation for Execution
AfriWork should be built as a modular, scalable SaaS platform with a strong trust layer, polished onboarding experience, and a clear path from profile creation to paid work. The first release should focus on credibility, usability, and workflow reliability rather than feature breadth.

---

## 22. Summary
AfriWork is not simply a marketplace. It is a digital infrastructure layer for work readiness, trust, collaboration, and economic opportunity for African youth. Its success depends on combining practical product functionality with strong platform governance, credible verification, and long-term scalability.
